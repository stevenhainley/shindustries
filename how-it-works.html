sh. industries · how it works, in one scroll

One person. An AI operating system. One thing for sale.

Steven built SH on his own Mac: a system where different AIs do different jobs, remember what they learned, and check their own work. He reviews everything before it leaves. This page is the answer to "so what does it actually do?"

Q: How does it work?

Steven asks. The system routes the job. A person signs off.

Q: What does it actually do today?

Real jobs it ran this week

  • Found and qualified founder podcasts by reading their YouTube numbers: episode views against Shorts views, straight from the channel.
  • Wrote one specific note per show with the real numbers in it, and sent them by email.
  • Cut an 81-minute episode into three finished clips in about 90 seconds of machine time: transcript, moment selection, captions, speaker crop, loudness, size limits.
  • Kept the books on itself: a morning brief, a sales pipeline, a health check, and a log of every outside action it took.
Q: What are we selling?

SH Shorts: done-for-you clips for founder podcasts

A founder who records a weekly show sends one episode. Within 48 hours they get 8 captioned, vertical, ready-to-post clips of its best moments, each with a hook line and a post caption, in a private folder. Most of these shows get thousands of views per episode and under a hundred per Short. That gap is the product.

Q: How does it make money?

Service first, then the system behind it

1
Free sample

Three clips posted next to their own. The proof is the product; there are no testimonials to lean on yet.

2
Paid pack

$149 an episode. The machine cost is under a dollar; the real cost is about 30 minutes of review. The margin funds everything else.

3
Monthly

Once a show buys a second episode: four episodes a month at a flat rate.

4
The system itself

Every job SH runs for a client becomes a reusable workflow. When enough of those exist, the workflows and the operating system behind them are the next product.

Q: What are the rules?

Five things the system is not allowed to do

  • No invented numbers. Every figure in a note was read off the channel that day.
  • No sending from Steven's personal accounts. Email goes out from his Gmail with his say-so; LinkedIn, X and Discord stay his.
  • No spending until the business has earned it.
  • No client work leaves without a person watching it.
  • No building for its own sake. Last fortnight 58% of the AI budget went to the system working on itself; now it has to point at customers.
